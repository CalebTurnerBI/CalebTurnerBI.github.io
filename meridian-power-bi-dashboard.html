Meridian Regional Health Network Executive Dashboard

An executive reporting dashboard built around a fictional 14-clinic healthcare network, using 2025 operational data to bring encounter activity, reimbursement, access, and patient experience into a consolidated view.

Project Overview

The assignment called for an executive dashboard that could give Meridian's leadership a concise view of network performance. The report combines encounter volume, reimbursement revenue, no-show activity, patient satisfaction, clinic performance, payer mix, and state-level results.

The completed analysis describes approximately 60,000 encounters, $16.88 million in reimbursement revenue, an 8.2% no-show rate, and an average patient satisfaction score of 3.8 across the year. These are results from the supplied academic scenario dataset, not real patient or healthcare-provider performance.

Dashboard & Analysis Views

  • Executive KPI cards for encounter activity, completed encounters, no-show rate, reimbursement, satisfaction, and wait-time measures.
  • Clinic revenue compared with monthly targets.
  • Monthly reimbursement trend to identify changes across the year.
  • Payer-mix analysis and state-level revenue comparisons.
  • Clinic satisfaction reporting.
  • Interactive slicers for month, clinic, state, and service line.

The written analysis notes that Virginia contributed the most revenue among the three states represented, followed by North Carolina and Maryland. Commercial and Medicare were the largest payer categories in the supplied data.

Data Model & DAX

The model uses the Clinic Directory as a shared dimension connected to encounter, monthly target, and patient-satisfaction tables. A separate Date table supports time-based analysis. A TargetMonthDate field was created to connect monthly targets to the Date table so that month selections filter actual revenue and corresponding targets consistently.

Measures covered encounter counts, completed encounters, no-show rate, reimbursement revenue, revenue targets and variance, average satisfaction, and average wait time. The analysis also describes dollar and percentage revenue variance measures.

Data Quality Exercise

The EHR-C raw export was treated as a separate data-quality exercise. Preparation included standardizing column names and status labels, converting encounter dates using the United States locale, standardizing service-line capitalization, and assigning blank payer fields the value “Unknown.”

Review identified 80 repeated EncounterID values; one repeated row for each duplicate ID was removed. Missing wait-time values were retained rather than replaced with zero. The cleaned EHR-C query remained separate from the main encounter table to avoid unintentionally counting the same encounters twice.

Business Findings

  • The 8.2% no-show rate gives leadership a reason to investigate appointment access and compare patterns by clinic, month, and service line.
  • Monthly revenue fluctuated during the year; the analysis recommends investigating seasonality, capacity, payer processing, service-line mix, and data timing before drawing conclusions.
  • Revenue-versus-target comparisons can help prioritize follow-up with clinics showing sustained shortfalls.
  • The dashboard highlights where to investigate, but does not establish the causes of differences or justify operational changes on its own.
