Manufacturing Operations Intelligence Initiative

A proposed phased business intelligence pilot for TreeHouse Foods’ Northlake, Texas facility.

Caleb Turner · Full Sail University · Business Intelligence Technology · November 22, 2026

Report overview

This report proposes bringing selected production, quality, maintenance, and inventory information into a governed analytical environment and presenting consistent operational measures through Power BI. The objective is to help plant leaders and operational teams identify production losses, understand recurring downtime and quality patterns, and make decisions using comparable information.

Public company materials identify Northlake, Texas as a production location associated with TreeHouse Foods’ coffee business. Public sources do not disclose the facility’s internal software, reporting workflows, or current operating results. Internal systems and performance figures are therefore treated as items requiring validation.

Academic proposal disclaimer: This is a planning exercise, not a system deployed at TreeHouse Foods or a statement of verified operational results. All financial estimates and proposed targets are illustrative assumptions requiring validation by site leadership and Finance.

1. Organizational Profile and Business Pain Point

TreeHouse Foods is a North American manufacturer of private-brand food and beverage products serving retail grocery, warehouse and club, e-commerce, foodservice, industrial, ingredient, export, and contract-manufacturing channels. This business case narrows the proposed scope to a pilot at Northlake rather than assuming an immediate enterprise-wide deployment.

The proposed business pain point is limited operational visibility across production, downtime, quality, and maintenance information. In manufacturing environments, these data domains may be recorded in different systems, shift records, spreadsheets, or local reports. When definitions and reporting periods differ, supervisors and managers may spend time reconciling information before identifying causes of lost production.

The pilot would test whether consolidating approved production, quality, and maintenance data improves reporting timeliness and makes recurring losses easier to identify. Baseline measures should include report-preparation time, time from shift end to report availability, completeness of downtime reason codes, and the percentage of records requiring manual correction.

Firsthand operational perspective

My work at TreeHouse Foods’ Northlake facility, including my role as a machine operator and support for autonomous maintenance and continuous-improvement activities, has given me a practical view of the connection between equipment performance, production output, and the work teams do to address recurring issues. Information is most useful when employees and leaders can understand what happened, when it happened, and what action is needed. A report connecting production results with downtime and maintenance information could help teams investigate causes and track whether corrective actions worked.

This perspective is based on my manufacturing work context, not a claim that a specific reporting failure or software limitation has been formally documented. Current reporting processes and source applications must be confirmed with authorized site and IT personnel.

2. BI Business Justification and ROI Analysis

The investment is justified if it reduces the time required to produce trusted operational reports, helps identify avoidable production losses, and supports faster corrective action. A measured baseline is required before benefits can be claimed.

Illustrative annual benefit assumptions

  • Reporting efficiency: 20 hours per week of reporting and reconciliation effort avoided, valued at $35 per hour across 50 working weeks, or $35,000 annually. This is released labor capacity, not necessarily payroll reduction.
  • Quality and rework: $45,000 in annual avoided scrap, rework, and investigation costs after adoption. Finance must validate the assumption using approved cost data.
  • Throughput and downtime: $65,000 in annual contribution value from recovered productive capacity. This depends on operational changes, available demand, and Finance validation.

Total modeled annual benefit: $145,000.

3. BI Technical Architecture

The proposed architecture uses approved source extracts or interfaces, Microsoft Fabric Data Factory and/or Dataflow Gen2 for ingestion and transformation, OneLake and a curated Fabric Warehouse for governed storage, and Power BI for reporting.

LayerProposed role
Operational sourcesApproved production, quality, maintenance, inventory, and related operational data.
Data integrationFabric Data Factory and/or Dataflow Gen2 to ingest, standardize, validate, and transform data.
Storage and modelingOneLake and a curated Fabric Warehouse with governed definitions and documented data lineage.
Analytics and reportingPower BI semantic model and role-appropriate reports for operational users and leaders.

Initial reporting should focus on production output, downtime, quality losses, changeover duration, and schedule attainment. KPI definitions, refresh expectations, data ownership, access controls, and reconciliation rules should be agreed upon before dashboards are accepted.

This is a conceptual proposal, not a confirmed map of TreeHouse Foods’ internal systems or interfaces. Specific source connectivity and implementation details require IT and business approval.

4. Risk Assessment

  • Data access and security: Source access may be restricted or require approvals. Mitigation: use approved interfaces, least-privilege access, documented ownership, and IT/security review.
  • Data quality and inconsistent definitions: Teams may define downtime, output, or defects differently. Mitigation: assign metric owners, document definitions, profile data, and reconcile pilot results with approved reports.
  • Limited internal capacity: Production subject-matter experts, IT, or analysts may not be available. Mitigation: keep the first release small, assign part-time owners, and schedule testing around shift operations.
  • Financial risk: Costs may exceed assumptions or benefits may not materialize. Mitigation: validate estimates, separate one-time and recurring costs, track actual costs, and require a pilot gate before scaling.
  • Scope expansion: Requests for more plants, real-time monitoring, predictive maintenance, or enterprise reporting could increase complexity. Mitigation: define pilot boundaries and require sponsor approval for scope changes.

Proposed pilot targets to negotiate with site leadership include a 25% reduction in recurring report-preparation time, at least 95% completeness for required fields, and reconciliation of critical KPI totals to approved source reports. These are proposed targets, not current performance claims.

Conclusion

A focused manufacturing BI pilot could support process-driven manufacturing and continuous improvement by making selected operational information more consistent and actionable. The proposed solution combines approved source data, controlled ETL, a governed analytical store, and Power BI reporting.

The recommended decision is to authorize a limited discovery and baseline phase, not an enterprise-wide deployment. Further investment should depend on evidence produced at each project gate.
